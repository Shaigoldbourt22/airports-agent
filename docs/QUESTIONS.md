# Sample Questions

Answers computed from FAA CY2025 preliminary enplanements and BTS On-Time Jul/Jan 2025.
Every number below came out of the data, not out of prose.

## Which airports in New England are strong candidates for terminal expansion?

**PVD, score 66.4 of 100.**

| Airport | Score | CY25 enplanements | Growth | Gates | Enpl/gate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PVD | 66.4 | 2,117,401 | +6.7% | 22 | 96,245 |
| BOS | 50.0 | 21,021,153 | −0.3% | 107 | 196,459 |
| BTV | 44.3 | 709,881 | +5.9% | 15 | 47,325 |
| BDL | 27.9 | 3,283,397 | −0.1% | 26 | 126,284 |
| MHT | — | 685,594 | +8.3% | unpublished | — |
| PWM | — | 1,287,041 | +5.3% | unpublished | — |

Score is half enplanement growth, half enplanements per gate, min-max normalised.
Normalisation runs within the four scored New England airports only, so 66.4 is regional, not national.
Those measure terminal load; delay rate is excluded because it tracks airspace, not gates.
PVD leads on both: rising demand against the region's second-tightest gate loading.
BOS is tighter per gate but flat, and it opened Terminal E and the B–C Connector in 2023.
MHT and PWM are unscored: neither publishes a gate count, and no federal dataset carries one.

## Compare LA and Santa Ana airport congestion levels.

**LAX is more congested: 23.4% of July arrivals late, versus 22.3% at SNA.**

| | LAX | SNA |
|---|---|---|
| CY25 enplanements | 36,497,303 | 5,521,729 |
| Jul 2025 departures | 17,104 | 4,002 |
| Departures delayed 15+ min | 22.6% | 23.5% |
| Arrivals delayed 15+ min | 23.4% | 22.3% |
| Airline runways | 4 | 1 |

The gap is small, which is the finding: SNA moves a seventh of the traffic on one runway.
Per runway, SNA runs 4,002 departures against LAX's 4,276 — near parity.
LAX is constrained physically: north runways 700 ft apart, widening scrapped 2016.
SNA is constrained legally: capped at 11.8M passengers and 95 Class A average daily departures.
BTS shows 129 SNA departures per day, but that counts every reporting carrier, including
regionals; the 95 cap applies only to Class A noise-category slots, so the two are not comparable.

## What is the percentage of long haul flights out of Anchorage airport?

**9.5% in July 2025, 0.5% in January 2025.**

Of 2,284 July departures, 216 exceeded 3,000 miles; in January, 6 of 1,187.
The 19-fold swing is seasonal: Alaska summer tourism doubles total departures and the
long-haul east-coast routes run only in season, so both numerator and denominator move.
The cutoff is ours; no FAA, BTS, ICAO or IATA standard defines long haul.
Six routes clear it: DFW, JFK, ATL, IAH, EWR, IAD, all 3,043–3,417 miles.
Seattle alone is 746 July departures at 1,448 miles, a third of all traffic.
BTS covers US carriers only, so Condor's seasonal Frankfurt service is absent.
Cargo is excluded, though ANC is the top US cargo airport at 26.3B lbs landed weight.

## What is the unmet flight demand in SFO airport and why?

**9 arrivals per hour at peak: airlines schedule 48, SFO has never delivered more than 39.**

| | SFO | LAX |
|---|---|---|
| Peak scheduled arrivals/hr | 48 | 51 |
| Best hour ever achieved | 39 | 50 |
| Sustained ceiling (95th pct) | 32/hr | 41/hr |
| Gap at peak | 9 | 1 |

LAX schedules to what it can fly; SFO schedules 23% above its best-ever hour.
In July 2025, 609 arrivals were scheduled into hours already above the sustained ceiling.
Cause: parallel runways 750 ft apart, closest of any US hub, a 1950s layout.
That spacing blocks independent parallel approaches under modern separation rules.
The demand does not vanish, it converts to delay: 28.6% of arrivals late, worst of ten airports measured.
Cancellations stay low (1.5%), confirming this is suppressed throughput, not lost flights.
Widening needs Bay fill, priced at $2.2B in 1999 and blocked on environmental grounds.
